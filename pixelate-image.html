How to pixelate part of a photo in three steps

  1. Add your image. It is processed locally — pixelation is a privacy tool, so keeping the file on your device is the point.
  2. Draw boxes over faces, plates, or text, then set the block size. Each block should be larger than the smallest detail you want hidden — if you can guess a letter, go bigger.
  3. Download. The mosaic is baked into the exported pixels. There is no hidden layer to peel away.

Choosing a safe block size

Pixelation replaces each block with its average color, destroying detail inside it. Safety scales with block size:

  • Faces: medium blocks are enough — identity lives in fine features, and the mosaic look is familiar from news broadcasts.
  • License plates & text: use coarse blocks, each wider than one character. Fine mosaics preserve letter shapes and can sometimes be partially reversed by AI upscaling.
  • Documents & IDs: skip the mosaic and black-box redact instead. When the content must be gone, gone-verifiable beats gone-probably.

Pixelate vs blur vs redact

Redaction destroys pixels completely — the safest option. Pixelation at a coarse block size is a close second and keeps the classic "censored" look. Blur looks most natural but is the weakest at low strength. All three run locally here; pick the strongest your situation calls for.

Frequently asked questions

How do I pixelate a face in a picture?

Upload the picture, draw a box over each face, choose a block size, and download. The mosaic is rendered into the exported pixels in your browser.

What block size hides a license plate?

Use chunky blocks — each block should be bigger than a single character on the plate. If you can still guess letters, increase the block size.

Is pixelation reversible?

Coarse pixelation destroys the underlying detail and cannot be reversed. Fine pixelation preserves shapes, so always use blocks large enough that the content is unrecognizable.

Does pixelating here send my photo to a server?

No. Everything runs locally in your browser. Your photos never leave your device.

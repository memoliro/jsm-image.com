How much blur is enough?

Blur works by averaging each pixel with its neighbors, destroying fine detail. The catch: mild blur preserves shapes — a lightly blurred plate can still be readable, and AI deblurring keeps improving at guessing what was there. Practical guidance:

  • Faces in casual photos — medium blur is fine and looks natural.
  • License plates and text — go heavy, or the characters survive. When in doubt, pixelate or black-box redact instead.
  • Documents and IDs — do not rely on blur at all. Solid redaction is the only verifiable option.

Blur vs pixelate vs redact

All three hide information, with different safety levels. Redaction (solid black) destroys the pixels completely — the gold standard. Pixelation at a coarse block size is nearly as safe and keeps a hint of context. Blur looks the most natural but is the weakest of the three at low strength. Pick the mildest option your situation allows — and when the stakes are high, pick the strongest.

Frequently asked questions

How do I blur a face in a photo?

Upload the photo, draw a box over each face, set the blur strength, and download. The blur is baked into the exported pixels locally in your browser.

How strong should the blur be to hide a license plate?

Stronger than you think. Light blur can sometimes be partially reversed by AI deblurring. For plates and text, use a high strength — or switch to solid redaction or heavy pixelation.

Can blurred areas be unblurred later?

Heavy blur cannot be perfectly restored, but mild blur preserves enough structure for AI tools to make educated guesses. Treat blur as casual privacy; for documents and IDs use black-box redaction.

Does this tool upload my photo?

No. Blurring runs entirely in your browser. Your images never leave your device.

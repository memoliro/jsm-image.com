Why solid black beats blur for real redaction

Blur and pixelation look safe, but light blur can sometimes be partially reversed — modern AI deblurring keeps getting better at guessing what was underneath. A solid black box has no information left to recover: every pixel is gone, verifiably. Rule of thumb: documents, IDs, and anything legal → black-box redact; casual privacy (a face in a crowd) → blur or pixelate is fine.

Common things people redact

  • Mail and documents — names, addresses, account and ID numbers before posting photos of letters or paperwork.
  • License plates — in car-sale listings, dashcam clips, and street photography.
  • Screens — screenshots that caught private messages, emails, or dashboards in the background.
  • Faces — especially children, or anyone who did not agree to appear in a published photo.
  • Barcodes & QR codes — they encode data you probably did not intend to share.

Frequently asked questions

Is blacking out parts of an image truly permanent?

Yes — when done correctly. This tool paints solid black pixels over the selected areas and exports a brand-new image file. The original pixels are destroyed, not hidden under a layer, so nobody can remove the black boxes to see underneath.

What should I redact before sharing a photo?

Names and addresses on documents or mail, license plates, ID numbers, faces of people who did not consent, QR codes and barcodes, screens showing private data, and house numbers in real-estate photos.

Does redacting here upload my image anywhere?

No. Everything runs locally in your browser. The image is never sent to a server — exactly what you want when the whole point is removing sensitive information.

Redact vs blur vs pixelate — which should I use?

Black-box redaction is the safest: the information is verifiably gone. Heavy blur and strong pixelation are usually fine, but light blur can sometimes be partially reversed with AI deblurring. For high-stakes content, use solid redaction.
